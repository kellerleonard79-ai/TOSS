# TOSS Inc. — Design Toolkit

A quick reference for how the site should look and feel. Keep every new page, graphic, or printed piece consistent with this.

## Color Palette

| Swatch | Name | Hex | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🟣 | **Plum Purple** | `#6F3BA6` | The brand color. Headlines, buttons, links, icons — anything that should feel like "TOSS." |
| 💜 | **Lavender** | `#D3BFEA` | A lighter, softer version of the brand purple. Backgrounds, section bands, and cards where full purple would be too heavy. |
| 🟢 | **Pastel Green** | `#C7E4BD` | Soft backgrounds, section bands, cards, subtle highlights. Calm and friendly, never loud. |
| 🌿 | **Green** | `#1E8449` | A brighter, grounded green for text, icons, and small details that need to read clearly against white. |
| 🟡 | **Warm Yellow** | `#F3DDA0` | Accents and small pops of energy — badges, highlights, illustration details. Use sparingly. |
| ⚪ | **White** | `#FFFFFF` | The main background. Keeps the page open, clean, and easy to read. |

**Rules of thumb:**
- No black, anywhere — for dark text, use a deep plum-brown instead of pure black (it's softer and still on-brand).
- White should always be the dominant color on a page. Everything else is an accent layered on top of it.
- Purple is the "call to action" color — the thing you want someone to click or notice should usually be purple.
- Lavender is purple's quiet sibling — use it where you want the brand color to show up without demanding attention (backgrounds, not buttons).
- Pastel green is for soft fills; the deeper green is for text and icons that need to actually be legible.
- Yellow never competes with purple for attention; it supports it.

## Font Pairing

**Headlines:** *Fraunces* (serif)
A warm, slightly editorial serif with real character. Use it for page titles, section headers, and big statements. It's what gives the site personality.

**Body text:** *Inter* (sans-serif)
Clean and highly readable at small sizes. Use it for paragraphs, buttons, navigation, and anything functional.

**Why this pairing works:** a characterful serif headline paired with a plain, quiet sans-serif body is a classic, universal combination — it reads as trustworthy and a little bit crafted, without being trendy or hard to read.

## Overall Feel

Warm, approachable, and a little bit handmade — like a community nonprofit, not a corporation. Generous white space, soft rounded corners, and gentle color rather than sharp contrast or heavy black text.
